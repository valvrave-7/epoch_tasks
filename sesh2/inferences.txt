1. Karnataka pincodes naturally form geographic regions

The K-Means algorithm separated the pincode coordinates into 5 spatial zones based on distance.

Since K-Means minimizes the distance between data points and their centroids, nearby pincodes were grouped together automatically.
This indicates that:
- Pincode distribution in Karnataka is geographically structured.
- The clusters correspond to zones.

2. Dense population and urban regions are visible

Some clusters, especially in the central and southern regions, appear significantly denser.
These dense areas likely correspond to:
- Bengaluru region
- Mysuru belt
- Other urbanized parts of central Karnataka

Higher point density generally indicates:
- More pincodes
- Greater settlement concentration
- Increased urban development

3. Northern Karnataka appears more spread out
The northern clusters cover larger geographical areas while containing relatively lower density.
This suggests:
- Settlements are more spread out
- Pincode concentration per unit area is lower

This observation is visible because:
- Points occupy a wider area
- Spacing is larger

5. Presence of outliers

A few isolated points are visible near:
- Longitude ~80
- Latitude ~10

These outliers may represent:
- Incorrect coordinate entries
- Border-region pincodes
- Rare isolated locations
- Data-entry inconsistencies

K-Means still assigns these points to the nearest cluster even if they are geographically distant.


6. Cluster centroids represent regional centers

The black “X” markers represent cluster centroids.
Each centroid represents the mean coordinate position of all points in that cluster.
These centroids can be interpreted as the approximate “center of gravity” of each geographical region.

For example:
- The southern cluster centroid roughly represents the average location of southern Karnataka pincodes.
